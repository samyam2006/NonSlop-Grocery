The Blacklist Section 02 · Read the fine print

The food that is
trying to fool you.

The front of the package is an advertisement. The back is a confession. This is how to read the confession — and the products and brands that hope you never will.

01

What to avoid, no matter what the front of the box says

These are the categories that do the most damage for the least nutrition. No marketing claim, no "organic" version, and no influencer can rehabilitate them. When in doubt, this is the short list to walk past.

  1. Industrial seed & "vegetable" oils

    Soybean, canola, corn, cottonseed, sunflower, safflower, grapeseed and generic "vegetable oil." Chemically extracted, heavily refined, and the single most common ingredient in ultra-processed food. Cook with butter, tallow, ghee, coconut or olive oil instead.

  2. High-fructose corn syrup & added sugar in savoury food

    Sugar in a dessert is honest. Sugar in bread, pasta sauce, salad dressing, deli meat, crackers and "savoury" snacks is camouflage. It is added to make cheap food addictive, and it is nearly everywhere in the centre aisles.

  3. Ultra-processed "food-like products"

    If it is built from refined starches, isolates, gums, emulsifiers and flavourings — most cereals, most snack bars, most packaged bakery, most frozen dinners — it belongs to a category that tracks with almost every modern metabolic disease. The tell is a long list of ingredients that do not exist in a kitchen.

  4. Sugary drinks, "fruit" juice and sports drinks

    Liquid sugar with no fibre to slow it down. A glass of apple juice delivers the sugar of several apples and none of the reasons to eat one. This includes most smoothies, sweetened iced tea, energy drinks and vitamin waters.

  5. Artificial sweeteners marketed as the healthy fix

    Aspartame, sucralose, acesulfame-K. They swap sugar's calories but keep the sweet-signal that trains you to crave more, and the evidence on the gut is unflattering. If you want sweetness, a little real honey or fruit is the more honest trade.

  6. Processed meat cured with the cheap stuff

    The problem is rarely the meat; it is what is added to it — added sugar, corn-syrup solids, phosphates, MSG and fillers in cheap deli slices, bacon and sausage. Buy simple cuts, or read the label until you find a version whose list is just meat, salt and spice.

  7. Anything selling itself as "diet," "lite," or "low-fat"

    When the fat comes out, sugar, starch and additives go in to replace the flavour and texture. Low-fat is one of the most reliable warning labels in the store. Full-fat, unsweetened, and plain is almost always the better version.

  8. "Plant-based" ultra-processed meat and dairy analogues

    A burger engineered from protein isolate, seed oil, methylcellulose and flavouring is not a health food because it skipped the cow. It is an ultra-processed product with a virtuous costume. Eat real plants or real animals — not a factory's impression of either.

02

The secret ingredient names, decoded

Manufacturers split, rename and disguise the ingredients they would rather you not notice — most often sugar and seed oil. Here is the codebook. When you see these on a label, translate.

Sugar, wearing sixty different names

What it says on the labelWhat it actually is
High-fructose corn syrupCheap liquid sugar refined from subsidised corn; the workhorse sweetener of processed food.
Cane juice / evaporated cane juiceSugar. The word "juice" makes it sound like produce. It is sugar.
Fruit juice concentrateSugar with the fibre and water stripped out — used so a label can claim "no added sugar."
MaltodextrinA refined starch that spikes blood sugar faster than table sugar; a hidden bulk sweetener.
Dextrose · maltose · sucrose · fructoseAll sugars. The "-ose" suffix is the tell.
Brown rice syrup · agave nectar · coconut sugar"Natural" sugars marketed as healthy. Metabolically, still sugar; agave is especially fructose-heavy.
Barley malt · rice syrup · corn syrup solidsMore sugar, listed separately so no single "sugar" appears near the top of the ingredient list.

The oldest trick: split one sugar into four differently-named sugars so none of them individually ranks high enough to top the list — even though, added together, sugar is the main ingredient.

Seed oil & additives, in disguise

What it says on the labelWhat it actually is
Vegetable oilAlmost always refined soybean or canola oil. "Vegetable" is the friendliest word for it.
"Contains one or more of…"A blend of the cheapest seed oils available that week, so the manufacturer never has to commit to one.
Mono- & diglyceridesA processed fat / emulsifier that can hide trans fats the "0g trans fat" claim doesn't have to disclose.
Natural flavoursA legally vague term covering dozens of manufactured flavour compounds. Real food does not need added flavour.
Autolyzed yeast · yeast extract · "spices"Common ways to add MSG-type glutamate without printing the letters MSG.
Carrageenan · cellulose gum · guar / xanthan gumTexture-fixers that let manufacturers replace real ingredients (cream, egg, fat) with water and air.
Caramel colour · Red 40 · Yellow 5 · titanium dioxideCosmetic dyes with no nutritional purpose; several are restricted or banned in other countries.
03

Fake-healthy foods, exposed

These are the products that have convinced a generation they are eating well. Each one hides behind a health halo. Turn it over and the truth is on the back.

THE HEALTH HALO

Flavoured & "low-fat" yogurt

Marketed as a probiotic health food. Many fruit-on-the-bottom and low-fat tubs carry more sugar per serving than a chocolate bar. Fix: plain, full-fat yogurt or kefir; add your own fruit.

THE HEALTH HALO

Granola & "healthy" cereal

The word "granola" implies virtue. Most are oats bound together with sugar and seed oil, then toasted — dessert in a breakfast bowl. Fix: plain oats, or eggs.

THE HEALTH HALO

Protein & "energy" bars

A candy bar with protein powder and a fibre isolate added so it can print a health claim. Long ingredient lists, sugar alcohols, seed oils. Fix: a boiled egg, jerky with a clean label, or a handful of nuts.

THE HEALTH HALO

Smoothies & cold-pressed juice

Sold as a salad you can drink. Most are a large dose of liquid fruit sugar with the fibre removed or blitzed into irrelevance. Fix: eat the fruit; blend with fat and protein if you must drink it.

THE HEALTH HALO

"Multigrain" & "wheat" bread

"Multigrain" and "made with whole grain" are unregulated flourishes. The base is usually refined flour dyed brown with caramel colour. Fix: "100% whole grain" as the first ingredient, or sourdough.

THE HEALTH HALO

Veggie chips & "veggie" straws

A picture of vegetables on a bag of potato starch, seed oil and a whisper of vegetable powder. Nutritionally, they are chips. Fix: actual vegetables, or nuts.

THE HEALTH HALO

Trail mix & "healthy" snack packs

The nuts are real; the candy, yogurt-coated bits and sweetened dried fruit smuggled in beside them are not. Fix: plain raw or dry-roasted nuts, unsweetened.

THE HEALTH HALO

Gluten-free packaged food

"Gluten-free" says nothing about health. Gluten-free cookies are still cookies — often with more sugar and starch to compensate. Fix: foods that were never grain to begin with.

THE HEALTH HALO

Plant-based meat & "milk"

Ultra-processed protein isolate, seed oil, gums and flavouring — or nut-water thickened with oil and gums. A costume, not an upgrade. Fix: real meat, or real whole plants; whole milk if you tolerate it.

THE HEALTH HALO

"Sugar-free" & "keto" treats

Sugar swapped for sugar alcohols and sweeteners, wrapped in the same seed-oil-and-isolate formula. A processed product with better marketing. Fix: just eat less of the real thing.

THE HEALTH HALO

Flavoured oatmeal & instant packets

Plain oats are a real food. The instant flavoured packets are half sugar by the time you finish the "maple & brown sugar." Fix: rolled or steel-cut oats, plain.

THE HEALTH HALO

Bottled dressings & marinades

Even "olive oil" dressings are usually mostly soybean oil, with sugar and gums. Fix: olive oil, vinegar or lemon, salt. Ten seconds, no additives.

04

The marketing words that legally mean nothing

These phrases are on the front of the package precisely because they are unregulated or nearly so. They are designed to buy your trust without earning it.

"Natural"
Almost entirely unregulated for most foods. It can sit on a product full of corn syrup and seed oil without breaking a single rule.
"Made with real fruit / whole grain"
"Made with" can mean a trace amount. A cereal "made with whole grain" may be mostly refined flour and sugar.
"No added sugar"
Often true only because the sugar arrived as "fruit juice concentrate" — chemically identical, differently labelled.
"A good source of fibre / protein / vitamin D"
A single isolated nutrient sprayed onto an otherwise poor food. Fortification is not the same as nourishment.
"Free-range" / "cage-free" (eggs)
Better than nothing, but loosely defined. "Pasture-raised" is the meaningful term for eggs; the rest is often marketing.
"Lightly sweetened" / "less sugar"
Comparative claims with no fixed standard. Less than what? Usually less than something absurd.
"Plant-based"
Describes the source, not the processing. Sugar is plant-based. So is seed oil. So is most junk.
"Superfood" (on a package)
A pure marketing word with no legal definition. Real superfoods rarely need to shout — see the next guide.

If you read one thing on this site

The entire trick is that fake food advertises and real food does not.

A tomato has no marketing budget. Neither does a lamb chop, a dozen pastured eggs, a tin of sardines or a jar of raw honey. The loudest claims sit on the products that need them most — and the quietest corner of the store, the one selling food that spoils, is where your health has always been. Turn the package over.
